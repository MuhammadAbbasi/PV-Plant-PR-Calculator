1. Executive Summary & Core Principle

The Performance Ratio (PR) evaluates the overall operational efficiency and quality of a solar photovoltaic plant. It quantifies how effectively the plant transforms the solar resource received on the panel plane into usable electrical energy, completely independent of seasonal or weather-driven irradiance fluctuations.

Fundamental Concept — Readable Definition IEC 61724-1 Standard
Performance Ratio (PR) =
Actual Electrical Energy Delivered   [kWh]
Theoretical Energy Supported by Solar Irradiance   [kWh]
× 100%
In plain words: "Out of all the kilowatt-hours the panels could theoretically have produced given the exact amount of sunlight that fell on them, what percentage was actually delivered?" A 100% PR represents a physically perfect system with zero thermodynamic, wiring, soiling, or inverter losses. A well-designed, healthy utility-scale facility like Mazara 01 typically operates in the range of 82% to 85%.

In industrial utility operations, external events outside the operator's control—such as transmission grid-ordered active power curtailments (Terna/dispatching instructions) or external grid disconnections—depress actual generation. To ensure that contractual performance guarantees (Allegato 9.1) are assessed fairly without masking equipment defects, two complementary PR metrics are computed:

  • Raw Performance Ratio (PRraw / PRtotal): Pure actual-vs-theoretical output with zero adjustments. Reflects what was delivered to the grid.
  • Compensated Performance Ratio (PRcomp): The contractual guarantee benchmark. Adds verified energy lost to external grid restrictions and outages back into the numerator.

3. Step 1: Solar Resource & Reference Irradiation (Href)

Solar irradiance is continuously sampled every 15 minutes by two secondary-standard pyranometers aligned in the Plane of Array (POA): POA1 located at Station TX1 and POA3 at Station TX3.

3.1 Conversion from Instantaneous Power (W/m²) to Energy Density (kWh/m²)

Each sensor reading arrives as instantaneous power flux (Gi in W/m²). Over a 15-minute interval (Δt = 0.25 h), the energy density Hi is obtained via:

Readable Formula — 15-Minute Solar Irradiation Integration Step 1.1
Irradiation   Hi   [kWh/m²] =
Irradiance   Gi   [W/m²]
1,000   [W/kW]
× 0.25   [hours] =
Gi   [W/m²]
4,000
Why divide by 4,000? Dividing by 1,000 converts Watts into Kilowatts. Multiplying by 0.25 hours converts power into quarter-hour energy: 0.25/1000 = 1/4000.

3.2 Dual-Sensor Fusion & Failure Recovery

To determine the single reference irradiance for each interval, the system supports two algorithms:

  • Arithmetic Average (Standard IEC 61724-1): The plain arithmetic mean:
    Havg, i = ( HPOA1, i + HPOA3, i ) ÷ 2
    Single Sensor Fallback: If one pyranometer fails or is zero while the other is operational, the operational sensor is used directly (Href = max(HPOA1, HPOA3)) to prevent halving the true sunlight.
  • Conditional MAX (`condmax` — Conservative Default): Evaluates sensor discrepancy:
    Discrepancy   δi = | HPOA1, i − HPOA3, i | ÷ Havg, i
    Hraw, i = max(HPOA1, i, HPOA3, i)   if δi > 10% (sensors diverge)
    Hraw, i = Havg, i   if δi ≤ 10% (sensors agree)
    Engineering Rationale: Shading, localized soiling, or sensor degradation causes a pyranometer to read artificially low. Averaging an erroneously low reading depresses the solar denominator, which artificially inflates PR. Selecting the higher sensor is conservative and contractually defensible.

3.3 Low-Light Noise Threshold Gate (Column I)

Readings under low irradiance (< 50 W/m²) are set to zero to remove dawn, dusk, and night sensor noise:

Readable Formula — Reference Irradiance Gate Excel Column I
Href, i = Hraw, i   (if Hraw, i × 4,000 ≥ 50.0 W/m²)   |   0.0   (if Hraw, i × 4,000 < 50.0 W/m²)
The surviving quarter-hour values form Column I of the daily calculation sheet. Their daily sum gives the total usable solar fuel:
Total Daily Solar Fuel (Σ I) = Hday = Σi=196 Href, i   [kWh/m²]
Excel:=IF(selected_poa*4000 >= $BA$7, selected_poa, 0)   |   Daily Sum: SUM($I$15:$I$110)

4. Step 2: Actual Energy Generation (Eactual)

Energy generation is tracked at two key boundaries:

  1. Inverter Level (Einv): Sum of active power for each inverter k over each interval i:
    Einv, total = Σk=136 Σi=196 [ Pk, i (kW) × 0.25 h ]   [kWh]
  2. Revenue Meter Level (Emeter): Cumulative readings (M in MWh) from the SATAC grid injection meter:
    Emeter, i = ( Mi − Mi-1 ) × 1,000   [kWh]
    Self-Healing Anomaly Repair: Any missing intervals, zeros, or non-monotonic meter drops are automatically detected and repaired via linear interpolation between the nearest valid boundary readings.

The Raw PR measures actual inverter generation against the ideal unconstrained output supported by the sun:

× 100%

6. Step 4: Contractual Degradation Model (Allegato 9.1)

Solar panel efficiency slowly decreases with age. Under contractual guarantee Allegato 9.1, the baseline PR target degrades by 0.4% per year (compounding annually) from the commercial start date (February 2025). Contractual years run from February 1 to January 31.

Readable Formula — Contractual Target Degradation Allegato 9.1
PRtarget(month, n) = PRbase, PVSyst(month) × ( 1 − 0.004 )n
Where n is the number of completed contract years since start:
  • Year 1 (Feb 2025 – Jan 2026): n = 0 → Factor = (1 − 0.004)0 = 1.000000 (no reduction)
  • Year 2 (Feb 2026 – Jan 2027): n = 1 → Factor = (1 − 0.004)1 = 0.996000
  • Year 3 (Feb 2027 – Jan 2028): n = 2 → Factor = (1 − 0.004)2 = 0.992016
  • Year 4 (Feb 2028 – Jan 2029): n = 3 → Factor = (1 − 0.004)3 = 0.988048

7. Step 5: Energy Loss Classification & Quantification

To evaluate whether equipment is performing to technical standard, energy lost due to external grid restrictions and outages is quantified. The theoretical expected power for inverter k under unconstrained solar conditions is:

Readable Formula — Theoretical Unconstrained Power Baseline Pexpected
Pexpected, k, i = min [ ( Gref, i/1,000 × Pdc, k × PRtarget ),   ( Pac, cap × 0.876 ) ]
Explanation: The power the inverter should make is solar irradiance × inverter DC capacity × degraded target PR, capped at the usable AC physical ceiling (320 kW × 0.876 ≈ 280.32 kW).

Each interval for each inverter is evaluated under three mutually exclusive loss mechanisms to eliminate double-counting:

1. DOWNTIME LOSS (Ldt)
Condition: Irradiance is up (Gref ≥ 50 W/m²) AND inverter power is zero/tripped (Pk, i < 1.0 kW).
• Sub-case A (Siblings Running): If other inverters on the same transformer are active (Pavg, TX > 1.0 kW):
Ldt, k, i = Pavg, TX, i × 0.25 h   [kWh]
• Sub-case B (Station/Plant Down): If all inverters on the station are down (Pavg, TX ≤ 1.0 kW):
Ldt, k, i = Pexpected, k, i × 0.25 h   [kWh]
2. CURTAILMENT LOSS (Lcurt)
Condition: Inverter is producing (Pk, i ≥ 1.0 kW) AND grid limit ratio is restricted (Rlimit, i < 0.875).
Lcurt, k, i = max( 0,   Pexpected, k, i − Pac, cap × Rlimit, i ) × 0.25 h   [kWh]
Anti-Double-Counting Guard: Curtailment loss is strictly zero when an inverter is offline (Pinv < 1 kW). During a full plant trip, the grid command reads ~0. Requiring Pinv ≥ 1 kW prevents booking both downtime and curtailment for the same interval, which would erroneously push Compensated PR over 100%.
3. RAMP / RECOVERY LOSS (Lramp)
Condition: Interval immediately adjacent to a full plant outage (t − 1 or t + 1), inverter is producing (Pk, i ≥ 1.0 kW), and uncurtailed (Rlimit ≥ 0.875).
Lramp, k, i = max( 0,   Pexpected, k, i − Pk, i ) × 0.25 h   [kWh]
Captures the shoulder energy lost while inverters synchronize and ramp up after a trip.

Total inverter loss for the interval is: Lk, i = Ldt, k, i + Lcurt, k, i + Lramp, k, i.

8. Step 6: Compensated Performance Ratio (PRcomp)

The contractual Compensated PR restores all verified external losses into the numerator:

Readable Formula — Compensated Performance Ratio Contractual Guarantee Benchmark
PRcomp =
Actual Generated Energy  +  Sum of All Recoverable Losses   [kWh]
Nominal Plant DC Capacity [kWp]  ×  Daily Reference Irradiation [kWh/m²]
× 100%
PRcomp =
Σk=136 Σi=196 ( Pk, i × 0.25 )  +  Σk=136 Σi=196 ( Ldt, k, i + Lcurt, k, i + Lramp, k, i )
12,625 kWp × Σi=196 Href, i
× 100%
Significance: Isolates the true technical health of the plant equipment. A correct PRcomp will sit near design expectation (~82–84%) even during heavy outage or curtailment days, proving that the shortfall was caused by grid events rather than defective plant hardware.

10. Multi-Day, Monthly, and Annual Weighted PR Aggregation

Critical Engineering Rule: Multi-day, monthly, or annual Performance Ratio is never the simple arithmetic average of daily PR percentages. Averaging percentages is mathematically invalid because low-irradiance cloudy days (e.g. 1.5 kWh/m²) would distort high-irradiance sunny days (e.g. 8.0 kWh/m²).

The true aggregated Performance Ratio over any period of D days is the energy-weighted sum:

Period-Aggregated PR Formula (Month / Year)IEC 61724-1 Weighted Sum
PRperiod, comp =
Σd=1D [ Eactual, d + LTX1, d + LTX2, d + LTX3, d ]
12,625 kWp  ×  Σd=1D Hday, d
× 100%
This exact energetic weighted calculation is implemented in Row 36 of the monthly Mother file (00 PR_recalculation_{MESE}.xlsx), matching contractual audit requirements.

12. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Examples

Example A: Single 15-Minute Interval (i = 61, 15:15 CET, June 2026, Year 2)

  • Measurements: POA1 = 990.0 W/m², POA3 = 968.0 W/m².
  • Irradiation: HPOA1 = 990 ÷ 4,000 = 0.24750 kWh/m²; HPOA3 = 968 ÷ 4,000 = 0.24200 kWh/m². Mean = 0.24475 kWh/m².
  • Discrepancy: |0.2475 − 0.2420| ÷ 0.24475 = 2.25% < 10% → Href = 0.24475 kWh/m² (979 W/m² ≥ 50 → Valid).
  • Degraded June Target: PRtarget = 83.3% × (1 − 0.004)1 = 82.97%.
  • Theoretical Power (Inverter with 359.375 kWp DC):
    Ptheoretical = (979 ÷ 1,000) × 359.375 kWp × 0.8297 = 291.90 kW → Capped at 280.32 kW
  • Case 1 (Normal Operation): Inverter produces 278.0 kW.
    Eactual = 278.0 kW × 0.25 h = 69.50 kWh,   L = 0.0 kWh
  • Case 2 (Inverter Tripped): Inverter produces 0.0 kW, siblings average 278.0 kW.
    Eactual = 0.0 kWh,   Ldt = 278.0 kW × 0.25 h = 69.50 kWh
    Compensated Yield = 0.0 + 69.50 = 69.50 kWh
  • Case 3 (Grid Curtailment at 60%): Grid orders Rlimit = 0.60. Inverter produces 320 kW × 0.60 = 192.0 kW.
    Eactual = 192.0 kW × 0.25 h = 48.00 kWh
    Lcurt = ( 280.32 kW − 192.0 kW ) × 0.25 h = 88.32 kW × 0.25 h = 22.08 kWh
    Compensated Yield = 48.00 + 22.08 = 70.08 kWh (= Pexpected × 0.25 h)
